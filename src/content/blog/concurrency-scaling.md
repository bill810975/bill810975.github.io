<h2 class="unnumbered" id="abstract">Abstract</h2>
<p>AI progress has repeatedly expanded the ways in which computation can be converted into intelligence. Model scaling increases the capability acquired through training <span class="citation" data-cites="kaplan2020scaling hoffmann2022chinchilla">(Kaplan et al. 2020; <span class="nocase">Hoffmann et al.</span> 2022)</span>; time scaling extends the reasoning a trained model can devote to a problem <span class="citation" data-cites="openai2024reasoning muennighoff2025s1">(OpenAI 2024; Muennighoff et al. 2025)</span>. A further route is emerging as agents pursue multiple lines of inquiry, conduct complementary experiments, and build on one another’s discoveries. <strong>We propose <em>concurrency scaling</em> as a third axis of AI capability</strong>: expanding concurrent agents and workflows to raise the intelligence attainable from a trained system.</p>
<p>The opportunity is a higher ceiling on what AI can discover, derive, and construct. Parallel exploration increases the range of candidate ideas, while specialization and shared results let a team develop solutions that no individual trajectory has yet reached. Recent advances in algorithmic optimization, collaborative engineering, and mathematical research make this opportunity concrete <span class="citation" data-cites="novikov2025alphaevolve carlini2026compiler anthropic2026riemann">(<span class="nocase">Novikov et al.</span> 2025; Carlini 2026; Anthropic 2026c)</span>. Their growth patterns differ across tasks and forms of collaboration. Established benchmarks reveal part of this progress; longer research efforts expose capabilities whose execution and verification require much larger resources.</p>
<p>We place model scaling, time scaling, and concurrency scaling within a unified view of total model-lifetime compute. We propose several growth patterns as hypotheses for future experiments using ElasticWeave <a class="cite" href="#ref-elasticweave2026repo">(Li 2026)</a>. We then outline the concurrency-aware models, training infrastructure, serving systems, and data-center resource pools needed to pursue this frontier. The central question is how far intelligence can advance when more computation supports a broader search for better solutions.</p>
<h2 id="sec:model">How Model Scaling Works</h2>
<p>Model scaling turns additional training computation into reusable capability. Larger models and more data improve the operations on which later reasoning and collaboration depend. <a class="cite" href="#ref-kaplan2020scaling">Kaplan et al. (2020)</a> described approximate power laws connecting language-model loss to parameters, data, and training compute. These relationships made computational investment a basis for predicting model improvement.</p>

 C_{\mathrm{train}}\approx 6 P D,
 \label{eq:training}
\end{equation}</annotation></semantics></math> where <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>P</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">P</annotation></semantics></math> is the parameter count and <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>D</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D</annotation></semantics></math> the number of training tokens <span class="citation" data-cites="brown2020gpt3">(<span class="nocase">Brown et al.</span> 2020)</span>. The expression connects model size and data through a common resource: total training compute.</p>

These improvements established a reusable source of capability on which reasoning and agents could subsequently build.</p>

<p>This expansion continues alongside reasoning and agent research. Open-weight releases grew from the 405B-parameter dense Llama 3.1 <a class="cite" href="#ref-meta2024llama31card">(Meta 2024a)</a> to sparse models with 1.6T total parameters in DeepSeek-V4-Pro <a class="cite" href="#ref-deepseek2026v4card">(DeepSeek-AI 2026)</a> and 2.8T in Kimi K3 <a class="cite" href="#ref-kimi2026k3">(Kimi Team 2026)</a>. DeepSeek also reports pretraining on more than 32T tokens <a class="cite" href="#ref-deepseek2026v4card">(DeepSeek-AI 2026)</a>. By 2026, both model capacity and training data had reached scales far beyond the early language-model scaling studies.</p>
<h3 id="how-benchmark-difficulty-kept-pace">How benchmark difficulty kept pace</h3>
<p>As models improved, harder tests restored the ability to distinguish them. SuperGLUE followed progress on GLUE beyond non-expert human performance <a class="cite" href="#ref-wang2019superglue">(Wang et al. 2019)</a>; GSM8K introduced multi-step mathematics <a class="cite" href="#ref-cobbe2021verifiers">(Cobbe et al. 2021)</a>, on which Llama 3.1 later reported 96.8% with eight-shot chain-of-thought prompting <a class="cite" href="#ref-meta2024llama31card">(Meta 2024a)</a>. Harder questions need not require a larger evaluation allowance: a fixed model can receive the same number of attempts and tokens on a more demanding test. This made model-scaling progress visible while much of the computational investment remained in training.</p>
<h2 id="sec:time">How Time Scaling Works</h2>
<p>Time scaling increases the work a trained model can devote to an individual problem. Chain-of-thought prompting exposes intermediate reasoning; self-refinement and tool-supported correction add repeated attempts and checks <span class="citation" data-cites="wei2022cot madaan2023selfrefine gou2024critic">(Wei et al. 2022; <span class="nocase">Madaan et al.</span> 2023; Gou et al. 2024)</span>. Reasoning models such as o1 and DeepSeek-R1 learn to sustain this process, making inference compute a resource that can be expanded after training <span class="citation" data-cites="deepseek2025r1 openai2024reasoning">(<span class="nocase">Guo et al.</span> 2025; OpenAI 2024)</span>.</p>
<h3 id="the-single-agent-token-budget">The single-agent token budget</h3>
<p>The central resource is the reasoning budget of one agent. A larger budget lets the agent develop a derivation, inspect evidence, and revisit intermediate decisions before returning a result. Time-scaling studies and the reasoning-effort settings of released models make this budget an explicit control over the use of a fixed model <span class="citation" data-cites="muennighoff2025s1 openai2025gptoss">(Muennighoff et al. 2025; OpenAI 2025a)</span>.</p>

<p>For complete research projects, however, additional capability also comes from investigating more possibilities and carrying out complementary work. ARC Prize’s discussion of Astra points toward self-improvement and open-ended innovation as future evaluation targets <a class="cite" href="#ref-kamradt2026astra">(Kamradt 2026)</a>. The next scaling question is therefore <strong>how broadly a system can explore in pursuit of more capable solutions</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="sec:concurrency">How Concurrency Scaling Works</h2>
<p>Concurrency scaling raises a further question about the attainable ceiling of AI intelligence: what can a trained system accomplish when many agents explore and develop solutions together? Each agent can reason at length, while the team pursues alternative hypotheses, conducts complementary experiments, and reuses discoveries. Increasing concurrency expands the potential scope of this exploration. The opportunity is especially substantial on problems for which finding a successful approach is itself a major part of the work.</p>
<h3 id="expanding-the-search-for-better-solutions">Expanding the search for better solutions</h3>
<p>The team’s generated-token budget can be written as <math display="block" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>T</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">t</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">e</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">s</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">t</mi></mrow></msub><mo>=</mo><msub><mi>T</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">c</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">o</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">o</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">r</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">d</mi></mrow></msub><mo>+</mo><munder><mo>∑</mo><mi>i</mi></munder><msub><mi>T</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><mo>,</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{test}}=T_{\mathrm{coord}}+\sum_i T_i,
 \label{eq:tokens}
\end{equation}</annotation></semantics></math> where <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><msub><mi>T</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">T_i</annotation></semantics></math> is the budget used by agent <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>i</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">i</annotation></semantics></math> and <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><msub><mi>T</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">c</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">o</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">o</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">r</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">d</mi></mrow></msub><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">T_{\mathrm{coord}}</annotation></semantics></math> covers coordination, integration, and review. With <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>K</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K</annotation></semantics></math> agents and a per-agent budget <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>b</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">b</annotation></semantics></math>, agent computation grows approximately as <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>K</mi><mi>b</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">Kb</annotation></semantics></math>. Longer trajectories deepen the reasoning available to each branch; more branches expand the ideas and evidence available to the system.</p>
<p><strong>Independent exploration, complementary work, and shared discoveries</strong> are three ways to turn that computation into greater capability. Different branches can pursue approaches that would otherwise remain unexplored. Specialized agents develop distinct components or gather different evidence. A useful result then becomes an input to several further investigations. Sampling and tree search established the value of alternative reasoning paths <span class="citation" data-cites="cobbe2021verifiers wang2023self brown2024monkeys yao2023tot">(Cobbe et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2023; Brown et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2023)</span>; AutoGen, More Agents Is All You Need, and MacNet extended the study to collaboration and team organization <span class="citation" data-cites="wu2023autogen li2024agents qian2025macnet">(<span class="nocase">Wu et al.</span> 2023; Li et al. 2024; Qian et al. 2025)</span>.</p>
<p>A richer search can produce a better algorithm, a stronger mathematical result, or a working system beyond the reach of an earlier configuration. These are capability milestones. Their practical meaning is the expanded set of problems the AI can solve and the quality of solutions it can construct. As with extended thinking, straightforward tasks may already be within reach; the frontier appears on problems that reward further exploration.</p>
<p>Frontier-model interfaces are beginning to expose concurrency alongside reasoning depth. OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 release separates <code>max</code>, which extends sequential reasoning, from <code>ultra</code>, which coordinates four agents by default, with additional 16-agent comparisons on selected tasks <a class="cite" href="#ref-openai2026ultra">(OpenAI 2026a)</a>. This makes a larger coordinated effort an explicit way to seek a more capable answer from the same model family.</p>
<h3 id="sec:taskfamilies">Capability milestones across four task families</h3>
<p>The following cases illustrate what higher intelligence can look like as exploration expands. Each family offers both concrete outcomes and room to pursue more demanding achievements.</p>
<h4 id="a.-long-horizon-optimization.">A. Long-horizon optimization.</h4>
<p>A feasible implementation leaves a large space of better algorithms, memory layouts, schedules, and constructions. Agents can investigate these alternatives concurrently and use experimental feedback to develop the strongest candidates. Capability appears in the best solution the system can obtain. KernelBench provides correctness and performance tests for this kind of search <a class="cite" href="#ref-ouyang2025kernelbench">(Ouyang et al. 2025)</a>.</p>
<p>AlphaEvolve combines asynchronous model sampling with automated evaluation. It matched the best known constructions on roughly 75% of more than 50 mathematical problems and improved them on roughly 20%; one scheduling heuristic recovered an average 0.7% of Google’s fleet-wide compute resources <span class="citation" data-cites="novikov2025alphaevolve">(<span class="nocase">Novikov et al.</span> 2025)</span>. Cursor and NVIDIA reported a 1.38<math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>×</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\times</annotation></semantics></math> geometric-mean speedup across 235 B200 kernels relative to single-agent-optimized PyTorch baselines, using a planner to distribute and rebalance work over a three-week search <a class="cite" href="#ref-lin2026kernels">(Lin et al. 2026)</a>. The achievements are more efficient programs and new constructions: additional exploration changes the resulting solution.</p>
<p>The experiments that distinguish candidates are part of this capability. METR’s NanoGPT optimization study found that experimental compute accounted for about 70–90% of the cost of most trajectories with continuous access to four H100 nodes <a class="cite" href="#ref-metr2026expenditure">(Cunningham et al. 2026)</a>. A growing exploration process therefore needs both model-serving and experiment-execution resources.</p>
<h4 id="b.-recursive-self-improvement-rsi.">B. Recursive self-improvement (RSI).</h4>
<p>A system can direct exploration toward its own tools, search policy, and agent implementation. Parallel branches propose and evaluate changes; successful changes expand what subsequent agents can accomplish. The Darwin Gödel Machine ran two evolution branches at a time and improved SWE-bench performance from 20.0% to 50.0% over 80 iterations <a class="cite" href="#ref-zhang2025dgm">(Zhang et al. 2025)</a>. Here the outcome is an improved problem solver whose capabilities carry forward to later tasks.</p>
<p>Frontier labs increasingly use agents in the research that develops future systems. OpenAI reports that its researchers increasingly run several agents at once to draft research code, troubleshoot infrastructure, investigate failures, and monitor experiments <a class="cite" href="#ref-openai2026researchacceleration">(OpenAI 2026d)</a>. Anthropic’s automated alignment researchers generate methods, train models, and test the results, with improvements transferring to models up to 4.7<math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>×</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\times</annotation></semantics></math> larger than those used during development <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2026alignmentresearch">(Anthropic 2026a)</a>. These loops motivate an especially consequential hypothesis: concurrency may expand both current exploration and the capability available to the next round of exploration.</p>
<h4 id="c.-proofs-and-open-problems.">C. Proofs and open problems.</h4>
<p>Mathematical research offers alternative proof strategies, separate proof obligations, and reusable lemmas. Concurrent agents can investigate several routes while promising intermediate results focus later work. Anthropic’s zeta-function project raised a lower bound on the proportion of zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%, with about 60 subagents participating in its successful session <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2026riemann">(Anthropic 2026c)</a>. Its Fermat’s Last Theorem project used dozens of agents over 11 days to formalize an established proof in Lean <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2026fermat">(Anthropic 2026b)</a>.</p>
<p>OpenAI reports a Navier–Stokes finite-time blowup result with smooth forcing from approximately 10,000 concurrent agents. The campaign reused progress on Euler equations and reassigned agents toward the harder problem, reaching a solution after 88 hours and completing formalization and verification 17 hours later <a class="cite" href="#ref-openai2026navier">(OpenAI 2026c)</a>. Such projects make mathematical discovery and proof construction concrete landmarks for concurrency scaling. Shared advances can open further lines of inquiry, extending the reach of the whole research effort.</p>
<h4 id="d.-research-and-engineering-projects.">D. Research and engineering projects.</h4>
<p>Complex projects combine evidence gathering, design, implementation, and experimentation. Anthropic’s research system delegates investigations to workers with separate contexts and synthesizes their findings, reporting a 90.2% improvement over a single-agent baseline on an internal research evaluation <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2025research">(Hadfield et al. 2025)</a>. Google’s AI co-scientist distributes hypothesis generation, reflection, ranking, and refinement across specialized agents; selected biomedical proposals were tested by experimental collaborators <a class="cite" href="#ref-gottweis2025coscientist">(Gottweis and Natarajan 2025)</a>.</p>
<p>Engineering provides tangible system-level outcomes. Anthropic reports that 16 Opus 4.6 agents produced a Rust-based C compiler capable of building Linux 6.9 <a class="cite" href="#ref-carlini2026compiler">(Carlini 2026)</a>. Their separate workspaces, task claims, and shared commits supported complementary implementation and maintenance. The completed compiler illustrates a capability expressed through an integrated artifact. More demanding specifications and projects provide subsequent milestones to pursue.</p>
<h3 id="learning-to-expand-the-effort">Learning to expand the effort</h3>
<p>Concurrency becomes more useful as systems learn what to explore and how to combine the results. Cursor’s long-running coding system separates planners, workers, and a judge; planners can create sub-planners as the project expands <a class="cite" href="#ref-lin2026scalingagents">(Lin 2026)</a>. This makes the organization responsive to newly discovered work.</p>
<p>Kimi K2.5 learns to create and coordinate up to 100 subagents; K2.6 expands the announced capacity to 300 subagents and 4,000 coordinated steps <span class="citation" data-cites="moonshot2026parl moonshot2026k26">(Moonshot AI 2026b, 2026c)</span>. OpenAI’s Codex provides isolated environments, project threads, and worktrees for concurrent work on shared repositories <span class="citation" data-cites="openai2025codex openai2026codexapp">(OpenAI 2025b, 2026b)</span>. Together, these developments make broader exploration increasingly accessible to both models and users.</p>
<p>The research opportunity is to connect this expanding capacity to <strong>higher achievable intelligence</strong>. Coordination policies, tools, and reusable discoveries determine which new avenues can be developed. The next scaling experiments should trace the frontier of outcomes reached as more concurrent resources are brought to bear.</p>
<h2 id="sec:unified">A Unified View</h2>
<p>Model scaling, time scaling, and concurrency scaling place additional computation at different stages of capability formation. Training develops the model, extended reasoning deepens its work on a problem, and concurrent agents broaden the search for solutions. Together, they suggest a common question: <strong>how does attainable intelligence grow with computational resources?</strong></p>

<h3 id="growth-hypotheses-for-a-higher-capability-frontier">Growth hypotheses for a higher capability frontier</h3>
<p>Existing studies offer several empirical starting points. <em>Large Language Monkeys</em> reports nearly log-linear growth in coverage on mathematics and coding tasks as independent sample counts increase over the studied ranges <a class="cite" href="#ref-brown2024monkeys">(Brown et al. 2024)</a>. Across reasoning, coding, and writing tasks, MacNet reports a logistic-shaped rise in normalized aggregate quality as the logarithm of its collaboration-network size increases <a class="cite" href="#ref-qian2025macnet">(Qian et al. 2025)</a>. Anthropic’s vulnerability-discovery swarm continued finding new vulnerabilities at a roughly constant rate over an extended campaign <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2026multiagent">(Anthropic 2026d)</a>. These observations concern sample count, network size, and accumulated work, respectively. They illustrate different ways in which expanding exploration can uncover further solutions.</p>
<p>For actual concurrency <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>K</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K</annotation></semantics></math>, we propose testing <strong>linear, sublinear power-law, logarithmic, and sublogarithmic growth</strong>: <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mi>K</mi><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K</annotation></semantics></math>, <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><msup><mi>K</mi><mi>α</mi></msup><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K^{\alpha}</annotation></semantics></math> with <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>α</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0&lt;\alpha&lt;1</annotation></semantics></math>, <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">log</mi><mo>&#8289;</mo></mrow><mi>K</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\log K</annotation></semantics></math>, and, for example, <math display="inline" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">log</mi><mo>&#8289;</mo></mrow><mo stretchy="false" form="prefix">(</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>+</mo><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">log</mi><mo>&#8289;</mo></mrow><mi>K</mi><mo stretchy="false" form="postfix">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\log(1+\log K)</annotation></semantics></math>. The empirical task is to identify which form describes the capability gain in each task family and over which range. A study would hold the model and per-agent allowance fixed while increasing concurrent exploration; complementary budget-controlled studies would compare the allocation of computation across depth and width.</p>
<p>The same task can eventually offer little remaining room for improvement, just as a short reasoning problem can be answered immediately. <strong>The frontier advances through more demanding goals</strong>: a better construction, a stronger theorem, a more capable successor agent, or a more ambitious engineered system. <strong>Our proposal is that concurrency provides another resource with which to pursue those higher ceilings.</strong></p>
<h2 id="sec:benchmarking">Studying Concurrency Scaling with ElasticWeave</h2>
<p>The next experiments should characterize the capability frontier reached by increasing concurrency. We propose two components: <strong>a platform that controls concurrent exploration</strong> and <strong>tasks with room for increasingly ambitious outcomes</strong>.</p>
<h3 id="a-platform-to-realize-concurrency">A platform to realize concurrency</h3>
<p>ElasticWeave organizes research, implementation, experimentation, and review into task-specific workflows <a class="cite" href="#ref-elasticweave2026repo">(Li 2026)</a>. Its outer dispatcher launches independent workflow owners under a concurrency limit. We will use this control point to vary team size, allocation, and result sharing while recording model calls, experiments, tools, and resulting artifacts.</p>
<p>Initial studies will expand concurrency at a fixed model and per-agent allowance. The central outcome is the quality and difficulty of the solutions reached: new successful constructions, improved optimization targets, completed proofs, and more capable successor agents. An iterative single agent, independent attempts, and collaborating agents provide three reference configurations. Fixed-total-budget and fixed-deadline comparisons will then examine how the resource allocation changes the reachable frontier. Repeated runs will characterize variation across tasks and searches.</p>
<h3 id="long-horizon-tasks-and-evaluation">Long-horizon tasks and evaluation</h3>
<p>The four task families in Section <a href="#sec:taskfamilies" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:taskfamilies">3.2</a> supply the capability outcomes. KernelBench, PaperBench, ResearchGym, and FrontierScience provide reusable tasks and evaluation components <span class="citation" data-cites="ouyang2025kernelbench starace2025paperbench garikaparthi2026researchgym wang2026frontierscience">(Ouyang et al. 2025; Starace et al. 2025; Garikaparthi et al. 2026; Wang et al. 2026)</span>. MirrorCode adds long-horizon software construction: its preliminary study reimplemented the 16,905-line <code>gotree</code> program, assessed with 2,001 end-to-end tests <a class="cite" href="#ref-epoch2026mirrorcode">(Adamczewski et al. 2026)</a>. Such environments can be extended with more difficult specifications and optimization targets.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation should follow what becomes achievable as resources expand.</strong> Alongside fixed task sets, prospective research problems can record improved bounds, reproducible discoveries, and newly completed systems. Successful intermediate results and their reuse will help explain how larger teams reached those outcomes. Execution and checking remain substantial parts of the experiment: PaperBench estimates $400 in agent API use for a 12-hour replication attempt and $66 for grading <a class="cite" href="#ref-starace2025paperbench">(Starace et al. 2025)</a>. The supplementary resource record provides further examples.</p>
<p>This program connects the growth hypotheses to observable capabilities while retaining space for achievements beyond today’s benchmark ceilings. ElasticWeave is the instrument for the study; the intended result is a characterization of concurrency scaling across tasks and resource levels.</p>
<h2 id="sec:requirements">What Concurrency Scaling Requires</h2>
<p>Pursuing a higher capability ceiling requires a computing system that can sustain many concurrent investigations. Models must learn to coordinate, training infrastructure must generate multi-agent trajectories, serving systems must schedule dependent model calls, and data centers must support both model computation and tool execution.</p>
<h3 id="models-trained-to-reason-about-concurrency">Models trained to reason about concurrency</h3>
<p>A concurrency-aware model should learn to decompose tasks, assign complementary work, and combine results. It needs to recognize dependencies, give workers the information they need, and choose between further exploration and verification. Training should reward the capability gained through broader exploration.</p>
<p>Kimi K2.5’s Parallel-Agent Reinforcement Learning provides a direct example. It trains an orchestrator to delegate work to dynamically instantiated, frozen subagents, with staged rewards for productive parallel work and critical-path-oriented step accounting <a class="cite" href="#ref-moonshot2026parl">(Moonshot AI 2026b)</a>. Coordination becomes a learned part of problem solving.</p>
<p>Anthropic’s experiments with 10–80 agents examine shared-code development and integration across model generations <a class="cite" href="#ref-anthropic2026multiagent">(Anthropic 2026d)</a>. These settings expose a useful training target: learning to develop solutions from other agents’ contributions as the team grows.</p>
<p>The next step is to learn from the contribution of each branch. Training traces should connect proposals, experiments, and checks to the results they enable, so credit can be assigned across the team. Experiments can then examine how these policies generalize to larger teams and different task dependencies.</p>
<h3 id="training-infrastructure-for-concurrent-trajectories">Training infrastructure for concurrent trajectories</h3>
<p>Concurrent training must manage model calls, tool execution, sandbox state, and rewards that arrive at different times. Agent Lightning separates agent execution from training through a transition interface <a class="cite" href="#ref-luo2025lightning">(Luo et al. 2025)</a>; HybridFlow orchestrates distributed training and generation <a class="cite" href="#ref-sheng2025hybridflow">(Sheng et al. 2025)</a>; AReaL decouples rollout generation from model updates <a class="cite" href="#ref-fu2025areal">(Fu et al. 2025)</a>. These systems provide foundations for training on complete agent workflows.</p>
<p>Concurrency scaling requires extending this support to variable-size teams and their dependencies. The infrastructure must checkpoint model and environment state, associate actions with model versions, and preserve intermediate results across long rollouts. Kimi K3’s report identifies persistent rollout and sandbox state as part of million-token agentic reinforcement learning <a class="cite" href="#ref-kimi2026k3">(Kimi Team 2026)</a>. Supporting such state at team scale would allow concurrent exploration to become a routine source of training experience.</p>
<h3 id="serving-concurrent-problem-solving">Serving concurrent problem solving</h3>
<p>Serving systems have adapted to larger models and longer generation through KV-cache management in vLLM, prefix reuse in SGLang, and prefill–decode separation in DistServe <span class="citation" data-cites="kwon2023vllm zheng2024sglang zhong2024distserve">(Kwon et al. 2023; Zheng et al. 2024; Zhong et al. 2024)</span>. Concurrent agents add a task-level scheduling problem: branches start together, pause for tools, resume with new evidence, and wait for shared results.</p>
<p>SemiAnalysis’s AgentX v1.0 makes this workload concrete: among 393 opt-in Claude Code sessions, 44% contain subagents; median request input and output lengths are 142k and 444 tokens, respectively <a class="cite" href="#ref-semianalysis2026agentx">(SemiAnalysis 2026)</a>. Long shared histories and short new outputs make retaining and routing reusable state central to serving the team.</p>
<p>The server therefore needs to understand which requests advance the task. A reviewer may complete the final step after many workers finish; an experiment may unlock several dependent branches. Agentix treats agent programs as scheduling entities and uses program-level execution history to prioritize model calls <a class="cite" href="#ref-luo2026agentix">(Luo et al. 2026)</a>. This moves scheduling toward the structure of the problem-solving process.</p>
<p>A concurrency-oriented interface should expose task identity, dependencies, deadlines, and pause, resume, and cancellation events. The server can then prioritize critical work, share common prefixes, and reclaim memory while agents wait on tools. <strong>Serving should preserve the team’s ability to explore and build on results.</strong> Task completion time and useful branch progress connect token-level scheduling to that objective. Artificial Analysis’s AA-AgentPerf measures how many active agents a deployment sustains while meeting per-agent service targets <a class="cite" href="#ref-artificialanalysis2026agentperf">(Artificial Analysis 2026a)</a>.</p>
<h3 id="data-center-resource-pools-and-serving-system-co-design">Data-center resource pools and serving-system co-design</h3>
<p>A concurrency-scaling data center must provision a complete problem-solving workload. The design question is the balance among GPU computation, CPU execution, memory, storage, and network capacity. Agent sessions move between these resources as they reason, run tools, wait, and resume. A workload study reports that tools and environments dominate or co-dominate latency in 5 of 10 applications, with sandbox working sets peaking at 28 GB per session <a class="cite" href="#ref-chang2026workloads">(Chang et al. 2026)</a>; CPU-centric analysis likewise identifies substantial host-side work <a class="cite" href="#ref-raj2025cpucentric">(Raj et al. 2025)</a>.</p>
<h4 id="size-pools-from-activity-and-resident-state.">Size pools from activity and resident state.</h4>
<p>GPU capacity follows active model calls and accelerator experiments. CPU capacity follows concurrent compilation, testing, simulation, and proof checking. Memory must support both active working sets and sessions waiting to resume; SSD pools retain repositories, datasets, checkpoints, intermediate artifacts, and colder context. Network bandwidth connects fan-out, shared results, and state migration. These demands need not grow in the same proportion. Their costs also evolve: Epoch AI estimates that HBM rose from 51.87% to 63.42% of AI-chip component spending between Q1 2024 and Q4 2025 (Figure <a href="#fig:components" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:components">[fig:components]</a>) <a class="cite" href="#ref-somala2026components">(Somala 2026)</a>. For example, a planning scenario with 10,000 resident agents and 1 GiB of private state per agent already requires about 10 TiB of aggregate storage capacity, before shared model and dataset storage, even if only a fraction of agents are currently decoding.</p>

<h4 id="manage-memory-and-storage-as-a-hierarchy.">Manage memory and storage as a hierarchy.</h4>
<p>Active KV state belongs close to GPU execution; reusable context and working sets can occupy DRAM or a memory pool; cold checkpoints and artifacts can reside on SSDs. Mooncake already organizes CPU, DRAM, and SSD resources around a distributed KV cache <a class="cite" href="#ref-qin2024mooncake">(Qin et al. 2024)</a>. Tutti studies the I/O path needed to make SSD-backed KV restoration practical <a class="cite" href="#ref-qiu2026tutti">(Qiu et al. 2026)</a>. For concurrent agents, this hierarchy must coordinate model context with sandbox state: checkpoint at suitable task boundaries, share read-only data, and restore the state needed by the next runnable branch.</p>
<h4 id="co-design-provisioning-and-scheduling.">Co-design provisioning and scheduling.</h4>
<p>A resource-rich GPU tier can still wait on overloaded tools, memory capacity, or slow checkpoint restores. Capacity planning should therefore use workload traces to estimate CPU core-seconds, GPU service time, resident bytes, SSD bandwidth and IOPS, and network traffic per task. The serving system can use the same measurements to admit teams, prioritize critical branches, and reserve capacity for verification bursts. The research objective is to determine how resource ratios and placement should change with concurrency and task mix, so that the available power and capital support more ambitious problem solving. <strong>Datacenter design becomes part of the concurrency-scaling methodology itself.</strong></p>
<h2 id="sec:conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Model scaling expanded what AI could learn; time scaling expanded what a trained model could derive through sustained reasoning. Concurrency scaling opens a further route: many agents can explore alternative ideas, develop complementary solutions, and build on shared discoveries. Algorithmic advances, substantial engineering artifacts, and reported mathematical results already demonstrate forms of capability that motivate this perspective.</p>
<p>We propose studying <strong>the higher intelligence attainable as concurrent resources grow</strong>. The relevant growth can differ across tasks and stages of discovery. Model-lifetime compute provides a common view of the investment, and linear, sublinear, logarithmic, and sublogarithmic hypotheses organize the next experiments. ElasticWeave will support these studies on increasingly demanding tasks. Concurrency-aware training, serving, and data-center design will make larger explorations practical. The longer-term objective is to expand the set of discoveries and solutions within AI’s reach.</p>
